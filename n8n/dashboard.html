Opportunity finder — where to buy premium right now

Ranks every instrument with real IV data by three things together: its category has an active or freshly-changed breadth call, its options are relatively cheap right now (cross- sectionally, against everything else tracked), and its price hasn't already run far from its own moving average (a simple "hasn't obviously peaked yet" check, not a scientific exhaustion signal — read it as a flag to look closer, not a guarantee). Matched against the ~15-session (~3 week) backtest evidence for that exact instrument and call, where enough history exists. Top 2 are highlighted; the rest of the ranked field is listed below them. This narrows down candidates — it does not tell you to trade one.

Panic Cycle Radar — where the panic cycle signal is strongest

Ranks every category by Socrates' own Panic Cycle % this week (platform_breadth's timing_array section, "Panic Cycle Signal Within 3 weeks" metric) — the same field that already drives the volatility flag elsewhere on this page, shown here as a magnitude instead of a boolean. Top 2 highlighted, cross-referenced with whichever optionable instrument tracks that category so you can see the panic-cycle reading and today's premium (cheap/rich) side by side. This does not know about specific calendar "target weeks" (a forward-looking Aggregate/Panic Cycle density table) or per-instrument Monthly Pattern labels (Turning Back UP, Still Under Pressure, etc.) — platform_breadth.csv doesn't carry either of those yet, so your Execution Rules 2 and 3 aren't encoded here. Send a sample export of those report sections and they can be added.

Breadth call vs. actual price trend

For each category's current breadth call, this shows what its canonical price proxy has actually done — trading above or below its own 50-day moving average, with the real number of sessions behind that average shown honestly. This page deliberately does not map a breadth call to a price direction — CAUTION/CONSTRUCTIVE/NEUTRAL are a composite of net reversal bias, net high/low, and panic-cycle exposure (per scorecard.py), not a forecast that price goes any particular way. Whether "CAUTION" means you'd expect this proxy to rise or fall is your own Socrates/Elliott Wave interpretation to bring to this reading, not something this dashboard asserts for you. Use this panel to check that interpretation against what the price has actually been doing, not as a signal on its own.

Convexity watch — breadth event + relatively cheap IV

Descriptive, not a signal to execute on: "watch" means this category's breadth call crossed a threshold (or tripped the panic-cycle volatility flag) this week, AND its optionable proxy is sitting in the cheaper half of everything currently tracked on IV. That's the same pattern behind the FXE/IBIT read from earlier — cheap premium on a category breadth just flagged. Price proxy figures (1wk/1mo % change, vs its own moving average) are shown alongside so you can see whether the move already happened or hasn't started — this page doesn't guess which.

Instrument decision panel

Pick a specific tradeable instrument and see its category breadth call, today's IV/skew (and rank once enough history exists), its own price trend, and what this exact symbol has actually done historically after this call — side by side, so you don't have to cross-reference four panels by hand. Defaults to FXE so the Euro/Yen worked example is one click away (switch to FXY to compare). This reads the same data as the rest of the page; it does not compute anything new and does not recommend a trade — see the caveat at the bottom of the panel.

Recent alerts

Nothing on this page is computed here — every number comes straight from v_scorecard_wow, alerts_sent, market_prices, iv_snapshots, v_iv_rank_latest, and v_price_moving_averages_latest in your Supabase project (the same tables/views the n8n workflows and local scripts read and write). Scores use the fixed formula in scorecard.py / v_scorecard: 0.4×net reversal bias + 0.4×net high/low + 0.2×panic-cycle exposure. CAUTION > 8, CONSTRUCTIVE < -8, otherwise NEUTRAL. IV/skew come from iv_snapshot.py — see its docstring for what "skew" does and doesn't mean here, and why its proxy symbols (FXE, IBIT) differ from the price proxies above them (EURUSD=X, BTC-USD). IV rank is a real percentile computed by v_iv_rank_latest from whatever iv_snapshots history has actually accumulated — it labels itself "building history (n=…)" instead of a percentile until at least 4 readings exist for that symbol, and gets more meaningful the more often you run update_iv.sh (or iv_snapshot.py directly). The Convexity Watch panel combines the same v_scorecard_wow call-crossing/volatility-flag data with each category's IV proxy's cross-sectional cheap/expensive rank (same ranking the Cheapest IV panel uses) — a category is only flagged "watch" when BOTH a fresh breadth event happened this week AND its IV proxy is in the cheaper half of everything tracked. It never asserts a price direction — the breadth score is a composite (net reversal bias, net high/low, panic-cycle exposure per scorecard.py), not a directional forecast — and it never recommends a trade; it only points out where a pattern worth a closer look is sitting. The instrument watchlist's moving averages and weekly/monthly % change come from v_price_moving_averages_latest (backed by v_price_moving_averages and v_price_change_windows) — display names, groups, and confidence tags come from the instruments table, which mirrors market_data.py's INSTRUMENT_UNIVERSE; a non-"high" confidence tag means that ticker mapping wasn't verified against live Yahoo data — see that file's docstring before trusting it for sizing. Any row in the Cheapest IV or Instrument Watchlist panels, and any Macro Series (FRED) card, can be clicked to expand its full history as a chart — fetched on demand (iv_snapshots, v_price_moving_averages, or fred_series filtered to that one symbol/series) rather than loaded upfront for everything. Price and volume are always drawn as two separate charts, never one axis with two scales — same for IV and skew. An expanded row stays open across the 60-second auto-refresh (re-fetched fresh each time) rather than silently collapsing. Macro Series (FRED) data comes from fred_series/fred_series_meta, pulled by fred_data.py from the St. Louis Fed's public fredgraph.csv endpoint — see that file's docstring for the same confidence-tagging discipline as market_data.py. Signal Backtest reads v_signal_backtest_summary, which joins each week's breadth call to its price proxy's return over the following ~15 sessions via price_proxy_map — read the n column before the average; see the view's comment in supabase_schema.sql for why. The Instrument Decision Panel pulls the same reads together per symbol rather than one canonical instrument per category: category breadth from v_scorecard_wow, IV/skew/ rank from iv_snapshots/v_iv_rank_latest, this symbol's own price trend from v_price_moving_averages_latest, and this symbol's own backtest history from v_instrument_backtest_summary (same idea as Signal Backtest above, but keyed by iv_proxy_map so FXE and FXY — or any two instruments in the same category — get their own separate numbers instead of sharing one). Currencies covers five real, optionable ETFs now (FXE, FXY, FXB, FXF, FXC), not just FXE — see iv_snapshot.py's docstring for why SGD/TWD/INR/CNY stay spot-only. Like every other panel on this page, it states facts, never a recommendation. The VIX headline card at the top of the page reads v_fred_series_rank_latest — VIX's own reading against its OWN history's percentile (same percent_rank() formula as IV rank, applied to fred_series instead of iv_snapshots), never compared directly against any single instrument's IV, since different asset classes sit on structurally different volatility scales. The Opportunity Finder below it ranks every instrument with both a live/fresh breadth signal and real IV data by a composite of three things: its cross-sectional cheap-IV rank, a small penalty if its category's call didn't actually change this week, and a small penalty if price is already more than 5% from its own 20-day moving average (OPP_EXTENDED_THRESHOLD_PCT) — all three genuinely combine into one score, rather than the first factor alone deciding and the other two never mattering. It shows the top 2 as full cards and the rest of the field that still qualifies below them; like every other panel here it narrows the field and states facts, it does not pick a trade. Skew across the Cheapest IV panel, category cards, and Decision Panel now reads in plain language ("puts expensive · calls cheap" instead of a bare signed number) via one shared skewLeanLabel() function, so all three panels describe the same skew the same way; the raw number is still there as a hover title. A "Breadth call vs. actual price trend" panel near the top shows each active category's canonical price proxy trading above/below its own 50-day moving average (sma_50/observations_50, same "however much real history actually exists" honesty as the 10d/20d averages) — deliberately without asserting what direction the breadth call itself implies, since CAUTION/CONSTRUCTIVE/NEUTRAL are a composite (net reversal bias, net high/low, panic-cycle exposure), not a price forecast; reconciling "does the measured trend match what I expected this call to mean" is left to your own Socrates/Elliott Wave read. The Instrument Watchlist and its drill-down chart now show a 50-day average alongside 10d/20d for the same reason. A "Panic Cycle Radar" panel below the Opportunity Finder ranks every category by panic_cycle_pct this week — Socrates' own "Panic Cycle Signal Within 3 weeks" reading from platform_breadth's timing_array section, the same field that already drives the volatility_flag boolean (>20%) elsewhere on this page, shown here as an actual magnitude instead. Top 2 categories are highlighted and cross-referenced with their mapped optionable instrument's current IV/skew, so a live panic-cycle reading and a cheap premium can be seen together; it doesn't track forward-looking "target week" calendars or per-instrument Monthly Pattern labels, since platform_breadth.csv doesn't carry either yet. market_data.py's tracked universe now includes IBIT (Crypto) alongside BTC-USD/ETH-USD, and fred_data.py now pulls three market-stress gauges alongside rates/CPI/VIX/dollar-index: BAMLH0A0HYM2 (ICE BofA high-yield credit spread), NFCI (Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index), and STLFSI4 (St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index) — all free, no-key FRED series via the same fredgraph.csv endpoint already in use; the MOVE index (bond-market VIX) isn't included since it's a proprietary ICE BofA series with no free fetchable source. Three tiles above the Macro Series (FRED) cards — US 2s10s, France−Germany 10Y, Italy−Germany 10Y — come from v_fred_spreads_latest, a plain subtraction of already-tracked FRED levels (DGS10/DGS2 and new Germany/France/Italy OECD 10-year yields), each spread's own latest reading computed independently since the US leg updates daily and the European legs update monthly. VVIX, the Cboe 1-Month Implied Correlation Index, and VSTOXX joined the tracked instrument universe the same way IBIT did; CDX IG/HY and iTraxx Europe Main have no free source anywhere found (IHS Markit/S&P Global-administered, paid-terminal only) — flagged, not faked. Auto-refreshes every 60 seconds — this is a static file, not a hosted page, so it only updates while you keep the tab open.
